8. KhetScore / FarmerChat / FarmCloud (India, Indonesia, Kenya)

Deployments:
• KhetScore (India, 2019) – AI-driven analytical tool for inclusive agricultural finance services
• FarmCloud (Indonesia, 2024) – AI chatbot for smallholder farmer advisory and sustainable agriculture
• FarmerChat (India, 2023) – AI-powered agricultural advisory for inclusive growth and climate resilience

AI Impact Commons Category: Agriculture

Description from AI Impact Commons

These deployments use AI to provide farmers with credit scoring, advisory services, and climate information via chatbots or analytical platforms. They aim to enhance financial inclusion, improve yields, and build climate resilience for smallholders.

Expanded Harms & Analysis

1. AI Credit Scoring Could Lock Farmers into Predatory Lending or Exclude Them

KhetScore's AI-driven credit scoring uses agricultural data (yield history, soil quality, weather patterns, transaction records) to assess creditworthiness. In India, where 7 out of 10 smallholders already lack access to formal credit, such systems could seem beneficial. However, if the scoring models are opaque and trained on historical data that reflects existing biases (e.g., against women, Dalits, or small plot holders), they could systematically deny loans to the most vulnerable. Alternatively, they might grant loans at exorbitant interest rates justified by "algorithmic risk assessment," pushing farmers deeper into debt.

2. Farmer Identity Systems Enable State/Corporate Control of Agriculture

To use these platforms, farmers must create digital identities that link their land (or plots), production data, and personal information. This creates a registry of farmers that governments or corporations could use to control cropping patterns, enforce compliance with corporate contracts, or target enforcement actions. In Côte d'Ivoire, similar digital identity systems risk land rights displacement; in India, they could facilitate similar land grabbing under the guise of agricultural "modernization."

3. Advisory Chatbots May Push Harmful Inputs or Monocultures

FarmCloud and FarmerChat use AI to advise on inputs (fertilizers, pesticides), planting schedules, and market opportunities. If trained on data favoring high-input commercial agriculture, they may encourage unsustainable practices that increase debt and degrade soil. They may also promote contracts with large buyers that lock farmers into unfavorable terms, effectively using AI as a sales tool rather than neutral advice.

4. Data Fuels Debt Collection Aggression or Land Seizure

Precise data on yields, income, and assets enables sophisticated debt collection. If a farmer defaults, lenders have granular data to seize assets or sue. Some platforms integrate with recovery agents, turning data into a tool for harassment. In India, farmer suicides are already linked to debt stress; AI-enhanced lending could exacerbate this.

5. Reinforces Debt Cycles for Smallholders

By making credit more accessible, these systems may encourage borrowing for inputs without ensuring profitability. The "inclusive" promise turns into inclusion in debt cycles. AI-driven reminders and pressure to adopt recommended seeds/chemicals can trap farmers in a dependency loop: borrow, plant, harvest, debt, repeat.

Systemic Pattern

This cluster of use cases demonstrates "private profit from public data" and "identity system proliferation." The data collected from farmers' fields and financial transactions becomes a proprietary asset for the platform company, while farmers remain vulnerable to the whims of algorithmic decisions and the financial sector.

Key Demands

  • Farmer data ownership: Farmers collectively own their agricultural data; platform companies are data stewards, not owners.
  • Transparent credit scoring: Explainable AI models; right to contest scores; cap interest rates tied to algorithmic assessments.
  • Right to be forgotten: Farmers can delete their data and withdraw from platforms without penalty.
  • Ban on predatory lending integrations: Prohibit linking AI credit scores to aggressive debt collection or loan sharks.
  • Community benefit sharing: If data generates profits, a share goes to farmer cooperatives or community funds.

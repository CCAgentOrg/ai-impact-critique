9. Thermalytix & AI Retinal Screening (India, multiple)

Location: India (and other countries)
Deployment: Thermalytix, QuRe.ai, other AI diagnostic startups
Scale: Mass screening programs in partnership with hospitals and governments
AI Impact Commons Categories: Healthcare, plus other entries like CerviScanner, Qure.ai

Description from AI Impact Commons

"Thermalytix: Privacy-Preserving AI for Breast Cancer Screening" uses thermal imaging and AI to detect breast cancer. It is touted as "privacy-preserving" because it does not require radiation or physical contact. Other retinal screening tools use AI to detect diabetic retinopathy, tuberculosis, etc., from eye scans. These systems aim to scale screening in low-resource settings.

Expanded Harms & Analysis

1. Creates Searchable Health Databases Repurposable for Discrimination

Every screening generates a biometric record (thermal image, retinal scan) linked to an individual. Even if the AI only returns a binary "positive/negative" result, the underlying images are often stored. These datasets become searchable biometric health databases. Without strong legal safeguards, they could be accessed by insurers to deny coverage, by employers to discriminate, or by law enforcement to track individuals with certain medical conditions.

2. "Privacy-Preserving" Claims Often Unverified Marketing

Thermalytix markets itself as privacy-preserving, but this refers to avoiding radiation, not data protection. The thermal images themselves are identifiable biometric data. How they are stored, who has access, and for how long is rarely transparent. The term "privacy-preserving" may mislead implementers into thinking no special data governance is needed.

3. Normalizes Population-Level Health Surveillance

Screening large populations as a matter of policy—especially using automated AI—normalizes the idea that the state and corporations have a right to biometric health data. This shifts expectations of privacy: from health being a private matter to something that is routinely scanned, recorded, and analyzed by external actors.

4. Integration with National ID Systems (Aadhaar) Risks Function Creep

In India, there is a strong push to link health services with Aadhaar, the national biometric ID. If AI screening results are linked to Aadhaar numbers, they become part of a broader identity profile accessible to myriad government and private services. This creates a unified surveillance system where health status can affect everything from welfare eligibility to employment.

5. Algorithmic Errors Can Cause Harm Without Remedy

Screening AI have false positives and false negatives. A false positive can cause severe anxiety, unnecessary further testing, and medical costs. A false negative provides dangerous false reassurance. Because these are "decisions" made by an algorithm, accountability is diffused. Who is liable: the hospital, the AI vendor, or the developer? Users often have no right to an explanation or human review.

Systemic Pattern

These use cases illustrate "unverified self-reported data" (the AI claims accuracy without independent validation) and "no harm mitigation discussed." They also show how "replicability" encourages spreading screening programs without adequate evaluation of downstream risks or opt-out mechanisms.

Grassroots science movement skeptical of commercial health technologies; advocates for public health over profit.

Coalition opposing privatization of health and advocating for comprehensive, equitable healthcare; critical of tech-only solutions.

Key Demands

  • Prohibit linkage to national ID: Health screening results must not be added to Aadhaar or any universal ID database.
  • Delete raw images after analysis: Only retention of final diagnosis with patient consent; no storage of biometric scans.
  • Independent validation: AI tools must undergo rigorous, transparent clinical trials across diverse populations before deployment.
  • Right to human review: Patients can request a second opinion from a human specialist at no cost.
  • Opt-out without penalty: No denial of other healthcare services for refusing AI screening.

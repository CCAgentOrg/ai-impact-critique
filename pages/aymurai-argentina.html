4. AymurAI: Structuring Judicial Data on Gender-Based Violence

Location: Argentina (with pilots in Mexico)
Deployment: DataGénero organization
Scale: Processing court rulings to build open dataset on GBV
AI Impact Commons Category: Other

Description from AI Impact Commons

"AymurAI: Structuring Judicial Data on Gender-Based Violence" is an open-source tool that uses Named Entity Recognition (NER) and regular expressions to extract key information from legal texts. It aims to help criminal courts collect and make available data on gender-based violence, addressing the lack of transparency in judicial treatment of GBV against women and LGBTIQ+ people in Latin America.

Expanded Harms & Analysis

1. Algorithmic Processing of Trauma Narratives

GBV court documents contain deeply personal, traumatic testimonies. Using AI to parse these texts raises ethical concerns about dignity and re-traumatization. Even if done with good intentions, the process of having an algorithm "read" and classify survivors' accounts can feel like a violation, especially if they have not consented to computational analysis.

2. GBV Data as High-Value Target for Malicious Actors

Databases containing names, addresses, and details of GBV survivors are attractive targets for abusers, stalkers, vigilantes, and state actors seeking to suppress cases. In regions with weak data protection (many Latin American countries), such a dataset could enable harassment, intimidation, or even physical violence against survivors who came forward.

3. Algorithmic Misinterpretation Harms Survivors' Cases

Natural Language Processing models can misclassify or mis-extract information, especially from nuanced legal language or emotionally charged testimony. Errors could misrepresent survivor statements, affecting ongoing cases or future legal strategies. The stakes are extremely high in GBV, where justice outcomes often hinge on precise details.

4. Lack of Proper Consent and Data Minimization

While AymurAI is developed by feminist researchers, it's unclear whether survivors whose cases are processed have been informed or given consent. Court documents are public, but that doesn't mean survivors expect algorithmic analysis. Data minimization principles—collecting only what's necessary—may be compromised when AI extracts all entities and relationships automatically.

5. Potential State Co-option and Weaponization

If the tool is adopted by state judiciaries, governments with poor GBV enforcement records or authoritarian tendencies could use the structured data to monitor, harass, or discredit survivors and advocates rather than to improve justice. The tool's open-source nature doesn't prevent misuse by bad actors.

Systemic Pattern

Even well-intentioned "feminist AI" projects can reproduce harm if they prioritize data extraction over community control. The AI Impact Commons listing presents AymurAI uncritically, without discussing risks of data centralization or the possibility of re-identification from "anonymized" datasets.

Key Demands

  • Informed consent: Survivors must be informed when their case data is used for AI processing and retain the right to withdraw.
  • Robust de-identification: Multi-layered anonymization with proven resistance to re-identification attacks.
  • Community data governance: Data storage and access controlled by feminist survivor-led bodies, not solely by developers or state.
  • Transparency about errors: Public reporting of false positive/negative rates, especially across different case types and demographics.
  • Opt-out for sensitive cases: Automatic exclusion of cases involving minors, sexual violence, or where survivor requests exclusion.

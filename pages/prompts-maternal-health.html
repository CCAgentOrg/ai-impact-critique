5. PROMPTS: Two-Way Maternal Health Messaging

Location: Kenya, Ghana, Nigeria, Tanzania
Deployment: Jacaranda Health (Kenyan non-profit) in partnership with governments
Scale: 3 million+ women in Kenya; pilots in Ghana, Nigeria, Eswatini
AI Impact Commons Category: Healthcare

Description from AI Impact Commons

"PROMPTS: AI-Enabled, Two-Way Messaging to Support Maternal and Newborn Health" is a service that provides personalized SMS advice in local languages to pregnant women and new mothers. It uses AI to classify questions and route urgent ones to clinical nurses, aiming to reduce maternal mortality by improving care-seeking behavior.

Expanded Harms & Analysis

1. Health Data + Telecom = Comprehensive Surveillance

PROMPTS runs on SMS, meaning all messages pass through telecom operators. Even if content is secure, metadata (who is messaging, when, frequency) reveals reproductive health status. Telecoms could retain this data, share it with governments (including law enforcement), or sell aggregated insights. In countries with weak data protection, this creates a surveillance vector targeting women's health.

2. High-Risk Pregnancy Data Enables Discrimination

If information about pregnancy complications, HIV status, or other sensitive health indicators becomes accessible to insurers, employers, or family members, women face risks of discrimination. Although Kenya has data protection laws, enforcement is weak. Leaks could lead to denial of insurance coverage, workplace retaliation, or family violence.

3. Shared Devices Breach Privacy in Patriarchal Households

In many settings, mobile phones are shared among family members. A woman receiving SMS about pregnancy symptoms may not have private access to her phone, exposing her health information to husbands, in-laws, or others who may disapprove of seeking care. This can lead to domestic conflict or forced discontinuation of the service.

4. Government Access Enables State Surveillance of Reproductive Rights

As PROMPTS integrates with national health systems, the state gains visibility into reproductive health trends and individual cases. In contexts where reproductive rights are under political attack (e.g., abortion restrictions), this data could be used to target women seeking services or to penalize healthcare providers. The platform's endorsement by the Kenyan government raises concerns about future co-option.

5. Data Repurposing Without Re-consent

The IDRC article notes PROMPTS is layering in "climate data, clinical history, and socio-economic information" to personalize messages. This function creep expands data collection beyond the original pregnancy support purpose. Women who signed up for health tips may not realize their data is being used for climate adaptation research or policy dashboards.

Systemic Pattern

PROMPTS exemplifies "government partnerships as legitimacy" and "vulnerable populations as data sources." The AI Impact Commons description celebrates scale but omits discussion of consent, data retention policies, or safeguards against misuse. The project also shows how "replicability" encourages spreading similar systems across Africa without localized privacy assessments.

Key Demands

  • Explicit consent for data uses: Separate opt-in for each purpose (health advice, research, policy dashboards).
  • No metadata retention: Delete call/SMS logs after minimal period needed for service delivery.
  • Device privacy protections: Messages should not appear on lock screens; encourage use of private messaging apps where feasible.
  • Ban on sharing with insurers/employers: Strong legal prohibitions on using PROMPTS data for underwriting or employment decisions.
  • Community oversight: Women's advisory boards to review data governance and algorithm updates.

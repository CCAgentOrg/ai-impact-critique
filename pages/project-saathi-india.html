7. Project Saathi: Scalable AI for Gender-Responsive Health (India)

Location: India
Deployment: Likely state health departments or NGOs (specific org not listed)
Focus: Women's reproductive health conversations via voice
AI Impact Commons Category: Healthcare

Description from AI Impact Commons

"Project Saathi: Scalable AI for Gender-Responsive Health" deploys AI systems to capture and analyze women's reproductive health conversations, often through voice-based interfaces. The system aims to provide personalized health guidance and scale access to gender-responsive care.

Expanded Harms & Analysis

1. Voice Datasets Weaponizable if Leaked

Reproductive health conversations are among the most sensitive personal data. A voice recording or transcript revealing pregnancy status, contraception use, abortion history, or reproductive health issues could be used for honor killings, family violence, or forced medical procedures in patriarchal contexts. In regions where women's reproductive autonomy is contested, such data breaches can be life-threatening.

2. Shared Devices in Patriarchal Households

Women in many Indian households have limited private access to mobile phones. If health conversations are conducted on shared devices, other family members (husbands, in-laws) may overhear or access recordings. This can lead to control over women's healthcare decisions, forced disclosure ofprivate health information, or retaliation for seeking autonomous care.

3. Cultural and Religious Backlash Restricting Access

If communities perceive that AI systems are collecting data on women's reproductive health, they may impose restrictions on women using these services. Traditional or religious leaders might forbid participation, framing it as exposure of family secrets or Western interference. This could ironically reduce women's access to vital health information.

4. Dialectal Variation Risks Misdiagnosis

Voice-based health AI trained on limited dialects may misinterpret women's speech patterns, medical terminology, or symptom descriptions. Misclassification could lead to inappropriate advice, delayed care, or false reassurance. For a woman with a high-risk pregnancy, an AI misunderstanding her symptoms could be fatal.

5. Data Used for Population Control or Discrimination

Aggregated data on reproductive health trends could be used by governments to target interventions in coercive ways (e.g., forced sterilizations, discrimination against women with certain health conditions). Historical abuses of reproductive health data for eugenic or population control policies haunt such systems.

Systemic Pattern

Project Saathi exemplifies "vulnerable populations as data sources" (women in patriarchal settings) and "no harm mitigation discussed." The AI Impact Commons listing celebrates scalability without addressing consent in hierarchical households or safeguards against data weaponization.

Key Demands

  • Explicit, context-specific consent: Separate consent for each use of reproductive health data; explain risks in local languages.
  • Data stored locally on device: No cloud storage; encryption at rest; user-controlled deletion.
  • No voice recording: Allow text-based or button-based interactions as alternative to voice.
  • Strong anti-discrimination laws: Explicit prohibitions on using reproductive health data for insurance, employment, marriage, or housing decisions.
  • Community oversight committees: Women from the community control data governance and algorithm updates.

2. Novissi Togo

Location: Togo
Deployment: Government of Togo, with support from World Bank, UC Berkeley, and others
Scale: 572,852+ beneficiaries (first phase); 5.7 million individuals profiled
AI Impact Commons Category: Other

Description from AI Impact Commons

"Novissi Togo: ML and phone data to deliver shock-responsive social protection" uses machine learning and mobile phone metadata to identify and deliver emergency cash transfers to informal workers affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. It combines satellite imagery, household consumption data, and phone records to predict poverty and target assistance.

Expanded Harms & Analysis

1. Permanent Telco-Gov Surveillance Infrastructure

Novissi created a system where telecom data (call detail records, location, spending patterns) is used to train ML models for welfare targeting. This establishes a precedent and technical infrastructure for ongoing surveillance. Once phone data is integrated into social protection, it becomes a permanent feature with function creep risks. The system normalizes the idea that phone behavior is legitimate ground for state scrutiny.

2. Algorithmic Opacity Risks Political and Social Bias

The ML model's training data and decision rules are not public. This opacity means targeting errors or biases cannot be challenged. In contexts with ethnic or political tensions, algorithms trained on historical data could systematically exclude opposition supporters or marginalized groups. The World Bank notes that Novissi's models achieved 90-93% accuracy, but that still leaves 7-10% misclassification, translating to hundreds of thousands possibly excluded or incorrectly deemed ineligible.

3. Conditional Welfare Tied to Digital Behavior

Novissi transformed welfare from a right into a conditional benefit determined by opaque algorithms. Future expansions could tie eligibility to additional digital behaviors: phone usage patterns, social connections, or even political engagement inferred from metadata. This creates a form of "algorithmic conditionality" that undermines the principle of universal social protection.

4. Data Repurposing and Function Creep

The same phone data infrastructure could be repurposed for law enforcement, political monitoring, or denying other services (e.g., passports, licenses) based on algorithmic scores. Without strong data protection laws and sunset provisions, the data collected for COVID-19 relief could permanently expand state surveillance capacity.

5. Chilling Effects on Communication

If people perceive that phone usage affects welfare eligibility, they may self-censor or avoid certain calls/messages. This chilling effect undermines freedom of expression and association, particularly among activists, journalists, and political opponents.

Systemic Pattern

Novissi demonstrates the "government partnerships as legitimacy" red flag: state endorsement enables rapid scaling without public debate. It also shows how "replicability" can be a vice: the platform is being promoted as a model for other African countries, potentially exporting surveillance infrastructure continent-wide.

Key Demands

  • Sunset clauses: Explicit time limits on data collection and use, tied to emergency duration.
  • Independent oversight: Civil society monitoring of algorithmic targeting criteria and outcomes.
  • Right to explanation: Affected individuals must be able to understand why they were included/excluded.
  • Prohibition on data sharing: Strict legal bars against sharing welfare targeting data with law enforcement or intelligence.
  • Community consent: Collective agreements before deploying ML-based social protection systems.
